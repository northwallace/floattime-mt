About Float ETA

A float planner built around one question: if we put in now, when do we get to the take-out?

Why this exists

Most float-time advice is a guidebook number: “figure 3 to 4 hours.” That number assumes some flow nobody wrote down. The same 9-mile stretch can take two and a half hours in June runoff and twice that in a bony September. Add a tube, a cooler, or a few stops to fish, and the guess drifts even further.

Float ETA starts from today's water instead. River pages identify their USGS gauge or reviewed agency flow source when one is available. Missing readings do not hide reviewed maps or published guide times. Reference gauges are labelled, and gauges on another river are not used as this river’s flow or water temperature. Where a live timing model is established, its estimate changes with the flow. New listings may show access information and published guide times while calibration is reviewed.

How the estimate works

Our original reference-speed models have not been validated against a published set of trip measurements. A checked distance or access point does not validate the travel-time model. Treat estimates as rough planning guidance and allow extra time.

  • Live flow. Our server reads current discharge (cubic feet per second) and, where available, water temperature from the USGS Water Data API or selected Bureau of Reclamation and Colorado Division of Water Resources stations, then briefly caches the response so visitors share a reliable reading.
  • Flow to speed. Where a live timing model is established, it uses a reference raft drift speed and flow. Speed rises with discharge following the hydraulic-geometry relationship v ∝ Q0.34, so doubling the flow makes the boat about 27% faster, not twice as fast.
  • Your craft. Paddled kayaks and canoes run about 20% faster than a drifting raft. Tubes run about 28% slower. Paddleboards are close to a raft.
  • Fishing. Turn on “Stopping to fish” and the planner adds 25 minutes per river mile.
  • Daylight. We calculate sunset at the put-in and tell you the latest sensible launch time, or when you'll reach the take-out if you enter a put-in time.
  • Weather. Air temperature, wind, rain chance, and UV for the put-in come from Open-Meteo.

What it can't tell you

Float ETA is a planning tool, not a safety tool. It doesn't know about the tree that fell across the channel last night, a closed access site, wind on a slow stretch, or how long your group spends on a gravel bar. Mileages are approximate. Wood, ice, and high water can turn an easy stretch into a dangerous one. Scout what you can't see, wear a PFD, and check the state agency for closures.

That's also why the river reports section exists. If you see a strainer, fence, or diversion, post it so the next boat knows. Reports expire after 45 days unless someone confirms the hazard is still there.

Where the data comes from

  • Streamflow and water temperature: USGS Water Data API and Bureau of Reclamation Hydromet and Colorado Division of Water Resources
  • Weather: Open-Meteo
  • Sunset: calculated on the page from the put-in's latitude and longitude
  • Rivers and stretches: put together from public access sites and state boating information

Where we float

Float ETA covers Montana, Idaho, Oregon, Washington, Utah, Arizona, Colorado, Wyoming, California, Missouri, and Arkansas, with more states on the way. Each state page lists every river and stretch we track. Access maps are available on individually reviewed sections in these states. Locations marked “Area” describe an approximate access facility, not an exact landing. Additional sections and states remain in review. Some listings provide published guide times while live float-time calibration is still under review. Guide times retain their source conditions and are not adjusted for fishing, craft or current flow.

Who runs Float ETA?

Float ETA is an independent river-planning project. River details are assembled from public agency information and corrections from people who use these stretches. The goal is to make practical planning information easier to compare while being clear about what still needs to be checked locally.

Get in touch

Wrong mileage, a missing take-out, or a river we should add? Send a note on the contact page. Corrections from people who float these rivers make the site better for everyone.
