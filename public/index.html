How the math works

1

Live gauge data

Every river here is tied to a USGS streamgage. We pull the instantaneous discharge (cfs) and water temperature, refreshed every time you load the page.

2

Flow → speed

River speed rises with discharge. We calibrate each river's typical drift speed at a reference flow, then scale it using the hydraulic-geometry relationship v ∝ Q0.34.

3

Your craft matters

A paddled kayak outruns the current; a tube with a cooler lags behind it. We adjust the estimate for what you're actually floating in.

4

Beat the sunset

We compute sunset at the put-in and tell you the latest reasonable launch time, so you're not navigating strainers in the dark.

Float-time questions

Short answers to what people ask before they put in.

How long does it take to float a river in Montana, Idaho, or Oregon?

It depends on today's flow, the stretch, and your craft. A 9-mile Class I run might be 2.5 hours in a raft at a healthy cfs and closer to 4 hours on a tube or if you stop to fish. FloatETA converts live USGS discharge into an ETA for each listed stretch.

How does FloatETA calculate float time?

Each river is tied to a USGS streamgage. We take a typical raft drift speed at a reference flow, then scale it with hydraulic geometry (speed rises with discharge to the 0.34 power). Kayaks, tubes, and paddleboards get their own multipliers. Optional fishing adds 35 minutes per mile.

What CFS is good for floating?

There is no single good number. A river looks Prime near its reference flow, bony well below it, and pushy well above it. Use the condition badge on each river: Low & bony, Mellow, Prime, High & fast, or Runoff.

How much extra time if I stop to fish?

Turn on Stopping to fish and we add 35 minutes per mile on top of drift time. A 9-mile stretch gains about 5 hours 15 minutes.

Which rivers does FloatETA cover?

Montana, Idaho, and Oregon. Montana includes the Madison, Yellowstone, Gallatin, Big Hole, Blackfoot, Clark Fork, Bitterroot, and more. Idaho includes the Boise Greenbelt, South Fork Snake, Henry's Fork, Payette, Salmon, Lochsa, Clearwater, and St. Joe. Oregon includes the Deschutes at Maupin, the Rogue, McKenzie, Clackamas, Sandy, John Day, Grande Ronde, and Owyhee. Directories: Montana, Idaho, and Oregon.

Can I still launch if it is getting late?

The calculator uses sunset at the put-in and tells you the latest reasonable launch, or your take-out time if you enter a put-in. Leave a buffer — strainers after dark are a bad plan.
