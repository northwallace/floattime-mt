Oxbow Regional Park

Oxbow Regional Park provides a perfect launch point for water-based activities and forest hikes in the majestic Sandy River Gorge.

Page sections

Use the left and right arrow keys to move between sections.

Overview

Here, in 1,000 acres of river, forest and ridge, you’re sheltered from the rush of life in one of Oregon’s extraordinary places, Oxbow Regional Park on the wild and scenic Sandy River. Unplug for the day while hiking the park’s trails, picnicking near one of its play areas, or fishing in the river. Or book a campsite to extend your escape from the day-to-day.

Hours

Temporarily closed

Oxbow is temporarily closed as of Sept. 8, 2026 to replace the park's aging water system. The entire park, including the boat launch and equestrian path, is closed. The park is expected to reopen in early 2027.

Directions

Visiting by bike

If you're biking, travel east on bike lanes on Stark or Division streets or Powell Boulevard. Continue east on Division and follow signs to the park. Or ride the Springwater Corridor Trail and then head north on the Hogan Road bike lane to Division. Bikes are welcome inside the park on paved roads and some marked trails.

Plan your visit

Is there a parking fee? Are there restrooms? Find those answers and more.

Parking

The fee to park at Oxbow Regional Park is $5 per car or $7 per bus/van. Parking is free with an annual parking pass.

Buy a parking pass

Each picnic area has a large gravel parking lot, and small parking areas are at trailheads and river access points. There are spaces for parking off the main road before entering day-use areas. Some areas have designated accessible spaces while others, especially areas that are not paved, do not have designated accessible parking.

Even with multiple parking areas, spaces can fill up quickly at peak times, like summer weekends. Consider carpooling if going in a group.

Limited cell service

There is limited to no cell service and no Wi-Fi within Oxbow Regional Park. In case of emergency, use the call box located in front of the welcome center or at the campground shower building; 911 calls are free.

Restrooms

Oxbow has three types of restrooms: vault toilets, flush toilets and portable toilets. Flushing restrooms with running water for hand-washing can be found at the welcome center, near the Eagle picnic shelter and at the main campground facilities area. Vault and portable toilets are located at additional sites throughout the park. Some flushing restrooms have changing tables. The vault and flush toilets are wheelchair accessible but only some have grab bars. No restrooms are available in the elk meadow or ancient forest areas.

Picnic tables

There are five reservable picnic shelters at Oxbow Regional Park. Reservations are required for groups of 25 or more.

Reserve a shelter

Numerous picnic tables are scattered throughout the park. They are available on a first-come, first-served basis.

Things to do

Camping

Oxbow Regional Park is a popular spot for camping. Along with traditional drive-in tent campsites, it has a dozen RV campsites. The camp restrooms and showers, as well as some campsites, are wheelchair-accessible.

Learn more and reserve a spot

Fishing

Fish from the riverbank or by boat. Be sure to follow all Oregon Department of Fish and Wildlife regulations. Children younger than 12 don’t need a license.

Hiking and trails

Hike 12 miles of trails. Some trails follow a high ridge overlooking the Sandy River, with steep side trails to the beaches. Other trails wind their way through dense forests and meadows, including a loop through the park’s Ancient Forest — one of the closest old-growth forests to Portland. Trails begin at many day-use areas, at the campground and near the boat launch. For additional trail details and a description of a moderately challenging hiking route, visit the maps and guides section.

Nature

Wildlife thrives in the park, and people can follow their footprints in the sand as creatures like mink, raccoons and deer visit the river to drink. Since time immemorial, salmon have spawned and hatched in this river and its tributaries, in turn luring eagles and other birds of prey. Oxbow and protected lands around it offer an ideal home for large animals like elk, black bear and cougar.

Large predators

Large parks like Oxbow Regional Park can attract larger mammals, including predators like cougars and black bears. When predators are sighted, Metro posts alerts at the park. However, even when an alert is not posted, it is wise to behave as if large wildlife may be nearby.

Learn more about living with wildlife

Picnics and special events

Oxbow Regional Park has five spacious picnic shelters, suitable for large gatherings. Each is reservable. Reservations are required for gatherings of 25 people or more.

Reserve a shelter

River recreation

Many people seek to beat summer’s heat by visiting the nationally designated Wild and Scenic Sandy River. It is a popular spot for tubing, boating and kayaking.

Be aware that the glacier-fed waters of the Sandy can be dangerously cold, even when the air temperatures are warm. They also have hidden hazards like steep drop-offs, strong currents and underwater snags. Always wear a personal flotation device when recreating on or around natural water. In the summer, Metro stocks life jackets that you can borrow for free during your visit.

Learn more about water safety

Planning your Sandy River float or paddle

To ensure your trip is both fun and safe, please plan ahead.

  • Two of the most popular floats are from Dodge Park to Oxbow or from Oxbow to Dabney State Recreation Area.
  • Different parks are open different hours. Plan ahead to ensure all shuttle vehicles can be accessed at the end of a trip.
  • The City of Sandy provides detailed information about the Sandy River Water Trail, including maps, paddle summaries, safety guidelines and more.

  • The Sandy River Water Trail Paddle Guide includes estimated paddle times and difficulty ratings for various sections of the river. Please keep in mind that estimated times are for paddling, not floating, and do not include time spent shuttling between sites. Floating can take hours longer than paddling, especially during the summer, when water levels can be significantly lower.

  • Watch out for underwater hazards, such as logs and large rocks. Observe posted hazard signs.

Trail guides

This information is provided to help visitors decide if a site or trail fits their needs. It describes features and general conditions but does not guarantee that a place will work for everyone. Each person’s abilities and needs are different, so please use this only as a guide.

The descriptions provided here are based on conditions on June 2, 2025. Other routes may offer very different experiences. Rain, wind, heat, cold, plants, animals and people can change a trail surface quickly, so look out for obstacles even if you know the trail.

Trail facts at a glance

  • Surface: natural surface with ruts, rocks, roots and some sandy sections.
  • Width: 2 to 6 feet.
  • Type of trail: one-way or out-and-back.
  • Distances: one-way 1.3 miles, out and back 2.6 miles.
  • Obstacles: narrow trails, steep slopes, roots, rocks, drop-offs, bridge without rails, sandy sections, tree debris.
  • Sun and shade: The trail is a mix of full sun and shady areas; playgrounds and picnic areas are open to the elements.
  • Sounds: lots of bird sounds, occasional airplane sounds, sounds from the river rushing and campers in campground.

Suggested route guide

A one-way hike, 1.3-miles in length, starts at the Happy Creek day-use area and ends at the Dragonfly day-use area. A hike can vary in distance by hiking out-and-back for 2.6 miles or entering the trail from different day-use areas, making a hike as short as 0.25 miles. The trail has a natural surface with some sections of sand deeper than 2 inches. Portions of the trail are also very steep. There are multiple sections with many roots growing across the trail and some ruts caused by natural erosion. The trail also varies in width, in some places less than 3 feet. The trail sometimes splits where a hiker can take a different path leading down to the Sandy River. Wooden fences create a barrier between the trail and large drop-offs to the river. Pinecones, sticks, needles, and other debris from trees are scattered along the trail.

Safety and policies

Find out what is and isn't allowed at Oxbow Regional Park.

Inclement weather, wildlife sightings and other alerts

Park operations may change due to events like inclement weather, sightings of large predators like cougar or bears, construction work or scheduled maintenance.

Don’t feed the wildlife

If wild animals eat human food, they can get very sick and even die. They can also get too used to people, which can lead to dangerous situations. The park is already full of their favorite foods. It’s best to sit quietly and enjoy watching the animals eat, hop, walk and rest in the forest. Please also make sure to securely dispose of your picnic leftovers to prevent attracting large wildlife like bears.

Erosion at Oxbow Regional Park

Due to ongoing erosion issues, parking spaces have been removed and the restroom building has been decommissioned near the boat launch. Be prepared to park elsewhere when visiting the boat launch area. Although the equestrian trail is open, please use caution and stay alert to changing conditions due to overhanging trees and erosion along the trail. Erosion is a natural process and cannot be prevented. Metro is working to find solutions for maintaining or relocating park amenities threatened by erosion.

Water safety

Natural water can have hidden hazards. Wear a life jacket while on the water.

Learn more about water safety

Pet policy

Pets are not allowed because they can damage sensitive habitat and threaten wildlife.

Read Metro’s full pet policy

Hunting policy

Hunting is not allowed at Oxbow Regional Park. 

Read Metro’s hunting policy

Drones

Drones, model planes, model boats and other remote-controlled vehicles are not allowed.

Geocaching

Geocaching is allowed with guidelines.

Read our geocaching guidelines

Smoking, vaping, alcohol

Smoking, vaping, and any other intoxicants are not allowed at Oxbow Regional Park. Alcohol is not allowed except with a special permit as part of a picnic shelter reservation.
