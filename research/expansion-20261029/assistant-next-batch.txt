FLOAT ETA — NEXT STATE / RIVER RESEARCH BATCH

Work on [STATE OR RIVER CLUSTER]. Prepare an evidence-backed import package;
do not publish, edit generated public assets, or approve maps automatically.
Use the supplied current catalog/RESEARCH_FORMAT.md, source-index.json and
access-index.json. The validator in that same package is the authority for
structure. Ask for those files if the package is missing them.

Prioritize a small connected set of useful public floats sharing landings.
Completeness means recording an honest decision for each candidate, not making
every candidate verified. Keep unrelated private landings and long stretches
with unresolved hazards out of a ready-for-visitors claim.

1. Identity first. Reuse permanent acc-... IDs. Keep the original identity for
   shared or cross-state landings. Preserve aliases. Identify the particular
   river landing, parking area and entrance separately; do not conflate them.

2. Establish public boating access and current opening status from the managing
   agency. Find agency GIS through an agency-owned page or map, then record the
   service, layer, public filter and stable feature identifier. An official pin
   can still be a campground, parking lot or dam rather than a river landing.
   A review must check that distinction and compare placement with imagery.

3. Use the registered agency-source pipeline when possible. Include only public
   fields needed for review. Save complete paginated results, query URLs, dates
   and checksums. Bind the reviewed access ID to its source feature. Never
   silently accept a partial response or promote a whole layer by proximity.

4. Exact points need dependable landing evidence. A documented public boating
   facility with only an agency park/area coordinate may be access-verified and
   displayed approximately. Keep candidate coordinates under research, never
   in a pending point's active lat/lon. Preserve conflicting coordinates and
   explain what would resolve them. A closure, uncertain permission or hazardous
   endpoint may require withholding even an approximate map.

5. Check the complete trip: downstream direction, confluences, dams, required
   exits, seasonal roads, permits and craft suitability. Distances must refer to
   the same named endpoints. Use published river mile markers or a published
   whole-trip length with citations. Do not substitute walking, shuttle-road or
   straight-line distance. Store uncertain distances as research evidence.

6. Guide times belong to sections with original craft/season/flow assumptions.
   Do not convert a multiday camping itinerary to hours afloat. New rivers use
   timing.mode unavailable and null refFlowCfs/refSpeedMph. An active flow gauge
   by itself does not create a live ETA calibration.

7. Check the gauge's identity AND recent numeric discharge (parameter 00060).
   The actual app calls the USGS Water Data API v1 continuous collection with
   monitoring_location_id=USGS-<station>, parameter_code=00060,00010, a seven-day
   UTC datetime interval and limit=10000. Save a dated response without any key.
   Temperature-only, seasonal/null, historical and discontinued records do not
   supply current discharge. Do not substitute flow from across a dam/diversion.
   Record gaugeContext for a known limitation and retain unavailable timing.

8. Dotted river paths are a separate review. Candidate geometry stays under
   research/routes/STATE with display, landOverlay, distanceEstimate and
   remainingTime false. A visually plausible line does not verify distance,
   takeout-bank side or GPS time remaining. Do not assume the widest branch is
   the route. Keep alternative channels and endpoint-offset evidence.

Deliver:
- Proposed per-river source records and state access registry, preserving IDs.
- Dated source manifest, raw agency snapshots and feature bindings.
- A section review queue distinguishing approximate access map, exact landing,
  route display, sourced distance, calibrated time and current opening status.
- Pending candidates and conflicts, with a concrete next action for each.
- Gauge audit results, including failed requests; no fabricated API responses.
- A change log identifying replacements, closures and changed distances.

Useful current examples:
- Russian River (Forestville): a new state with one county-backed summer float,
  approximate park markers, source-backed length and guide time, no live ETA.
- John Day: shared permanent landings and sourced full/shorter trip options.
- Boise: reviewed landing and river-left takeout; unsafe downstream extension held.
- Encampment: active replacement gauge with explicit reach limitations.
- Sandy: a documented closure overrides an otherwise plausible access map.

The application maintainer runs build_catalog.py, build_review_queue.py, source
binding checks, audit_gauges.py and browser checks, then deliberately reviews
section fingerprints. Successful import and tests do not authorize publication.
